1 · Start with run-time

Stop paying for light nobody is using.

Car parks, corridors, stairwells, foyers, storage areas and external spaces may need lighting available around the clock. That does not mean every fitting needs to operate at full output around the clock.

Sensors

Return to full output when movement is detected and reduce again after a set period.

Dimming

Maintain background light where needed without paying for 100% output continuously.

Schedules + timers

Match operation to actual use rather than a fixed 24/7 pattern.

The 2am test

If the common areas look almost the same at 2:00am as they do at the busiest part of the evening, there may be an unnecessary run-time problem.

2 · Read the bill

The bill shows what problem is actually costing money.

Once obvious run-time waste is understood, look at tariff structure, operating periods, peak demand and load profile before recommending larger capital projects.

Solar

Most useful where common-property daytime consumption is meaningful.

Battery

Most useful where the tariff and load profile create a measured opportunity to shift energy or reduce peak demand.

Simple rule

Don't buy a battery to solve a problem a timer could fix.
